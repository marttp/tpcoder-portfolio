## Why This Post Exists

- Frame this as a personal opinion shaped by backend, payment, and financial-service work—not a universal technology-selection framework.
- Start from a recurring problem: technology discussions often collapse into speed, popularity, or benchmark comparisons.
- Establish the central claim: a technology choice is also a business decision because somebody inherits its cost, risk, and operating model.
- Explain the intended scope: this is not a ranking of technologies and not another system-design walkthrough.
- Preview the questions the post will explore: original intention, problem fit, business consequence, ownership cost, AI-era economics, and career growth.
- State what remains open: the framework should evolve as the author gains more experience and encounters different environments.

## Technology Has an Intention

- Explain that a technology usually begins with a concrete problem and constraint, rather than an abstract goal of being “the best.”
- Separate the tool’s original design intention from an incidental benefit, popular benchmark, or current marketing narrative.
- Ask whether our problem and constraints resemble the environment the technology was designed for.
- Treat every technology as a bundle of assumptions and trade-offs, including the conditions under which it stops being useful.
- Describe the risk of carrying a choice forward only because a previous company, team, or respected engineer used it.
- Leave room for one personal decision to be added later as evidence, without turning the section into a technology comparison.
